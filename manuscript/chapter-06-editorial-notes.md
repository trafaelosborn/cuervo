## What the chapter does

The chapter opens with the September 1922 delayed train that brought business to Cuervo hotels and stores. It distinguishes passing traffic from the journeys, handling tasks and purchases that occurred locally. The narrative develops:

1. Directional passenger access in 1926: a Tucumcari day trip versus an overnight Santa Rosa pairing.
2. Santa Rosa's already concentrated retail activity in 1929 and Tucumcari's larger commercial scale.
3. Rittenhouse's unequal roadside provision in 1946, with the guide's scope explicit.
4. Separate road improvements and realignments, especially the 1952 southwest relocation before interstate construction.
5. The 1958 passenger-stop bracket, the independently bracketed TO designation and continued siding.
6. Rock Island ownership and carrier-specific rights, followed by the 1974 no-agent/billing arrangement.
7. Rail, trucks and local dealers within the 1947 El Rancho feed network.
8. Interstate access, the distinction between mainline traffic and customers, and the reported 2013 station.
9. Santa Rosa's different chronology, functions and sources of resilience.
10. The cumulative sequence and handoff to Chapter 7's economic synthesis.

The chapter uses the established institutional voice. Sentence diagnostic: mean 13.6 words, longest 28 under the simple punctuation split. This is an editing aid, not a formal accessibility score.
