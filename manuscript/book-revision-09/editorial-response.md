## Editorial decisions

The recurring problem was an observation followed by a qualification, then another statement of the same limit in the caption or the next paragraph. Where accurate terms such as reported, proposed, county, precinct, or advertised already specify the claim, the extra disclaimer has been cut. Possible mechanisms retain conditional language.

- Chapter 1 introduces the geography and argument without anticipating every later limit.
- Chapters 2 and 3 retain the distinctions between inventories, sales, profits and regional markets while dropping repeated warnings after examples.
- Chapter 4 keeps proposed subscriptions, repairs and teaching visibly prospective. Operator changes and different service histories no longer receive an additional methodological conclusion each time.
- Chapter 5 preserves census units, boundaries, sample restrictions and uncertain family links. It cuts repeated reminders that school attendance and relocation are different events.
- Chapter 6 explains passenger service, freight, agency and billing as separate functions. The DOT captions now describe their drawings without repeating the limits already stated alongside the plans.
- Chapter 7 states the cumulative interpretation directly. Its synthesis retains the central limit: feedback and reopening costs are plausible mechanisms, not a demonstrated causal chain explaining every closure.
- Chapter 8 lets the continuing uses of the locality carry the conclusion.

## Material limits retained

The text still distinguishes the precinct from the village, changing boundaries from constant-area comparisons, head counts from output, and prices from profits. The source notes continue to record technical limitations. The thesis and underlying historical findings are unchanged.
