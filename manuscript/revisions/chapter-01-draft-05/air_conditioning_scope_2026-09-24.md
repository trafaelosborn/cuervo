# Home air conditioning and the Cuervo argument

**September 24, 2026. Updated after the full research sweep.**

## Current assessment

Home cooling improved the possibilities for comfortable residence. The evidence does not establish it as a leading cause of Cuervo's loss of services, or establish a negligible total effect.

The [full research memorandum](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/research_evidence/home_cooling_2026-09-24.md) supersedes the earlier limited literature check. It adds visually checked census tables and definitions, a historical utility source, reproducible NOAA temperature calculations, and an expanded literature assessment with reading coverage recorded.

## What changed

- **Local uptake is documented at two geographic levels.** Guadalupe County reported AC in about 3.0% of occupied housing in 1960, about 29.0% of year-round housing in the 1970 AC distribution, and 45.2% of year-round housing in 1980. The occupied/year-round denominator change prevents treating this as a harmonized household series.
- **Santa Rosa has a direct 1980 observation:** 66.1% of 985 year-round units. Its population fell from 2,485 in 1970 to 2,469 in 1980, then to 2,263 in 1990. Uptake and population stagnation coexisted. This does not identify the no-cooling counterfactual.
- **The census excluded evaporative coolers in all three years.** Reported AC does not measure every form of cooling. Biddle also notes possible respondent misclassification in 1960.
- **Equal local costs remain unverified.** Housing, income, electricity and installation all matter. The 1940 United Power opinion supplies a Santa Rosa utility anchor for the 1937 tax dispute; a matching Cuervo connection chronology was not found.
- **The climate comparison has a concrete limit.** NOAA's Cuervo inventory has precipitation and snow observations but no maximum/minimum temperature series. Santa Rosa has historical temperature data. The sweep does not assign those readings to Cuervo.

## Manuscript decision

Draft 5 replaces the conditional-only opening passage with the Santa Rosa observation and a short explanation of its limits. It retains the accessibility revision and institutional Meridian voice. The fuller discussion belongs behind Chapter 7's comparison of explanations.

No extra archival collection is required for this bounded treatment. A claim that cooling caused migration would require evidence connecting a dated local difference to household decisions. An equipment count or first electric connection alone would not establish that.
