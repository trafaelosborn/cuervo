## Interpretation for this book

In-car cooling could change the inconvenience of a journey and the appeal of stopping for relief from heat. That offers a possible route from greater travel comfort to fewer stops in a particular small town. It is the book's inference, not an estimated historical effect. More comfortable journeys might also encourage additional trips. Local trade depends on both traffic volume and stopping or spending choices.

This distinction fits the existing question about which activities needed to take place in Cuervo. A road can remain accessible while the reasons for stopping change. The same reasoning could affect residents' willingness to travel farther for services. Neither application should be presented as an observed local behavioral change without appropriate evidence.

The postwar spread of automotive cooling is relevant to later roadside trade. It cannot explain the recorded 1911 inventory withdrawal or Hicks's 1922 decision. National adoption dates also cannot identify a local business closure date.

Building air conditioning is a separate question. Its possible influence on destination choice, accommodation standards or regional settlement must be distinguished from cooling inside cars. Evidence about aggregate Sunbelt growth cannot simply be transferred to the comparison between Cuervo and Santa Rosa.

## Placement and stopping rule

Chapter 1 now gives a brief, sourced introduction to automotive air conditioning and clearly labels the local mechanism as a possibility. Chapter 6, “Changing the connections,” is the appropriate place to compare road access, travel comfort and the need for roadside stops. This is a bounded addition to the existing transport argument, not a new compulsory research program.

If Chapter 6 needs a stronger local claim, first check relevant held travel guides, advertisements and travel accounts for a specific change in stopping patterns or reasons for stopping. An advertisement establishes an offered amenity, not its effect on trade. If no stronger evidence is available, retain the conditional explanation and avoid assigning a numerical share of decline to it.
