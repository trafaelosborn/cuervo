## What changed

The chapter now begins with the September 1911 Clipper's simultaneous reports of lumber-stock withdrawal, school construction and depot improvements. That juxtaposition introduces differing local functions before the familiar highway account. Contemporary ranching establishes the continuing productive territory through documentary evidence.

The first-person field opening, dog photograph, informal church account and selected Higgins family vignette have been removed from Chapter 1. Their removal does not alter the underlying research files. Relevant documentary evidence remains available to later chapters when it advances the settlement's history.

The chapter follows trade, institutional support, overlapping geographic units and economic mechanisms. Named historical actors remain where their documented decisions explain an arrangement: Hicks's stated political and financial purposes, for example, bear directly on newspaper support. Personal color is not their purpose.

Detailed source qualifications are concentrated in the twelve working notes. Material limits remain in the prose, including precinct geography, the meaning of a railway billing assignment and the distinction between a plausible mechanism and a demonstrated causal chain. Formation and the uneven fortunes of services remain better established than the allocation of all later losses among causes.

At approximately 2,900 main-text words, the revision is slightly below the provisional 3,000–4,500-word allocation. Compression serves this opening; no material was added simply to restore the earlier count. The full book's 40,000–60,000-word scope is unchanged. Longer analysis belongs in the allocated chapters, and Chapter 1 ends by handing formation to Chapter 2.
