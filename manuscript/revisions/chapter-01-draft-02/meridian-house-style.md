# Meridian house style

**Agreed September 24, 2026. Applies to the Cuervo manuscript and its reading proofs.**

## Editorial standard

**Economist prose, Axios editing, scholarly documentation.** These are editorial references, not an instruction to reproduce either publication's signature wording or layout. Meridian should have a recognizable institutional voice of its own.

The prose develops a connected argument for an intelligent general reader. The editing makes the consequential point easy to find. The documentation lets a specialist inspect the evidence and distinguish observation from interpretation.

## Voice

- Write in an institutional voice. Use Meridian branding and an institutional byline. Preserve named source attribution and photography credits separately.
- Avoid first-person narration, personal recollection, staged encounters and sentimental reflection. Begin with a consequential finding, historical problem or documented contradiction.
- Let people appear through decisions and actions supported by records. A proprietor's stated reasons can explain an enterprise; a decorative anecdote cannot carry a general conclusion.
- Use direct sentences and concrete verbs. Prefer plain language to abstract phrasing; explain the mechanism before naming the economic concept.
- Allow restrained wit when the evidence supplies it. Do not add jokes, sneer at historical actors or treat disagreement as ignorance.
- State the strongest supported conclusion. Keep uncertainty close to the claim it qualifies, without making every paragraph a catalogue of things the evidence cannot prove.
- Avoid repeated rhetorical contrasts, rhetorical questions, throat-clearing, inflated novelty claims and a closing moral in every section.

## Argument and pacing

- Give each paragraph one principal job. Start with the useful proposition; follow with evidence, interpretation and only the qualifications that affect it.
- Use connected paragraphs for chapter prose. Short paragraphs are welcome when earned; a succession of punchlines fragments an argument.
- Borrow Axios's insistence on the point, economy and clear hierarchy. Reserve labels such as “Why it matters” and extensive bullet lists for briefs, summaries or selected sidebars.
- Explain why a number matters and what it counts. A precise number does not justify a precise causal explanation.
- Introduce theory where it helps explain a historical arrangement. Neither the name of a model nor a resemblance to its predicted curve establishes that the model caused an outcome.
- Keep the whole settlement as the unit of the book's narrative. Develop a ranch, household or business only as far as it advances that account.
- End sections by advancing the argument. End chapters by giving the next chapter a substantive problem to solve.

## Evidence and scholarly apparatus

- Separate contemporary observations, retrospective reconstructions and the book's own inferences. Attribute each when the distinction affects the conclusion.
- Preserve material limits in the prose: geographic denominators, unobserved causal links and the difference between an administrative listing and actual activity.
- Put detailed reading coverage, archival locators, minor transcription caveats and alternative date conventions in notes where practical.
- A small case can establish that an arrangement existed. Claims about prevalence, magnitude or typicality require broader evidence.
- Report competing accounts fairly. The highway-centered interpretation varies among writers; do not manufacture a unanimous account to overturn.
- Do not invent dialogue, motives, interior states, building identities or continuity between isolated observations.
- Keep US spelling for this US historical project. British editorial references do not require British orthography.

## Visual presentation

Use the agreed high-end editorial direction: generous space, readable serif body type, restrained navy accents, strong hierarchy and careful image sequencing. Photographs and figures must contribute evidence or spatial understanding. Their captions describe what is established, with credit and provenance retained. The surviving fabric of a building cannot independently date a closure or establish its cause.

## Revision test

Before keeping a paragraph, ask: what does it establish; what supports it; why does the reader need it here? Remove repetition before adding length. The 40,000–60,000-word budget remains subordinate to the argument.

Chapter 1 draft 2 is the first application of this standard. Earlier first-person recommendations and drafts are superseded as guidance for the current manuscript.
