## Change

The user identified two weaknesses in giving household cooling a substantial role: relocating an established household entails expense and disruption, and room air conditioners offer an alternative to installing central systems. The chapter now states the resulting assessment directly in one paragraph. Cooling merits consideration but offers little explanation for Cuervo's loss of town services. The Santa Rosa observation remains context rather than an estimate of cooling's effect.

Note 12 adds the 1960 room-unit categories and retains the 1980 primary table, definition and checked population counts. The census already recorded 42 occupied Guadalupe County housing units with room equipment and none with central air. These estimates establish regional use, not installations in particular Cuervo homes or the size of those homes. The moving-cost argument is the book's assessment of a mechanism, not a measured cost comparison. The research summaries now reflect the same judgment: exact equality of installation costs is unnecessary, and an inability to prove zero effect creates no further research requirement. The existing literature assessment remains in the full memorandum.
