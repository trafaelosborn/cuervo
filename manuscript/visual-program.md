## Editorial direction

Continue the high-end 1990s editorial direction: generous space, readable serif typography, navy accents, restrained rules and clear hierarchy. Give images enough room for readers to inspect their evidence. Alternate photographs, source reproductions, maps and statistical figures according to the chapter's work.

Selected visuals can answer a question, establish a spatial relationship, show an observation, let the reader inspect a source, or contribute atmosphere, texture and pacing. Photographs do not each need to establish an economic claim. A broad photograph and a fine-grained document can serve different purposes on neighboring pages. Several similar ruined interiors should not crowd out the productive territory and commercial relationships discussed in the text.

## Production conventions

- **Photographs:** check the original at reproduction size, record credit and metadata, and use distinct wide views and details. Current images describe the photographed condition; captions need independent support for historical identities and dates.
- **Source pages:** retain source, date and page identity. Pair a readable crop with a small context view or a brief transcription where needed. Put editorial arrows and labels outside the archival image. Preserve the untouched source alongside derivatives.
- **Maps:** show scale, orientation and relevant boundaries where the source permits. Identify insets and schematic layouts. Keep historical and later annotation layers distinguishable.
- **Charts:** carry units, years, geographic scope and concise sources. Use consistent colors and real-dollar or indexed comparisons when discussing purchasing power. Show missing data and definition breaks. For price/population comparisons, prefer separate panels sharing a time axis over adjustable dual axes.
- **Reproduction:** retain editable SVGs for generated figures and high-resolution source masters. Confirm selected-image provenance and reproduction terms during the existing publication review.
