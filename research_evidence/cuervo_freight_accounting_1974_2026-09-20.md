# Cuervo freight accounting in 1974: no resident agent, billing assigned to Tucumcari

*Research date: September 20, 2026. Follow-up to the search for cattle shipping after the 1958 passenger-service change.*

## Finding

**Rock Island's Station Book, issued December 15, 1974, lists Cuervo with its own car-accounting, location, and revenue identifiers, marks it as having no agent, and assigns it billing number 8310, the number used by Tucumcari.** This is direct evidence of a published administrative arrangement for Cuervo long after passenger stops disappeared.

**An actual post-1958 Cuervo cattle shipment has still not been located.** The station book is an accounting and routing reference. It is not a dated waybill, a list of stockyards, a tariff explicitly accepting cattle at Cuervo, or a traffic return showing cars actually loaded. Its codes must not be treated as shipment counts or conclusive proof of continuing livestock service.

The distinction matters: the hypothetical arrangement of a station without an agent retaining a freight identity, with its billing assigned elsewhere, is now documented for Cuervo itself. Whether stock cars were supplied and collected there remains a separate question.

## Transcription of the relevant entries

The following are identifiers, not counts. An asterisk means that the book lists no agent at that station. The absence of an asterisk at Tucumcari is reported as an unmarked entry, rather than an independently verified payroll observation.

| Station | No-agent marker | Car-accounting / blocking code | Location code (SPLC) | Revenue / audit code | Billing code |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tucumcari | None | 16137 | 773150 | 8310 | 8310 |
| Montoya | Asterisk | 16158 | 773168 | 8353 | 8310 |
| Newkirk | Asterisk | 16169 | 774113 | 8354 | 8310 |
| **Cuervo** | **Asterisk** | **16178** | **774115** | **8355** | **8310** |
| Santa Rosa | Asterisk | 16196 | 774120 | 8357 | 8310 |

Source for all rows: printed C 15 / PDF 67. Cuervo's row was cross-checked against B 10; Santa Rosa against B 37. Cuervo appears on J 20 at mile 1149.5 from Chicago, between Newkirk and Los Tanos. This geographic check distinguishes it from similarly named places and confirms the relevant New Mexico main-line segment.

### What the billing code tells us

The column explicitly identifies an accounting billing station. Cuervo's entry uses Tucumcari's 8310 rather than its own revenue/audit identifier 8355. **The published assignment places Cuervo's billing with Tucumcari.** It does not show a particular rancher's phone call, completed bill, or method of transmitting paperwork.

The same pattern applies to Montoya, Newkirk and Santa Rosa. This is useful evidence of administrative consolidation along the corridor, including Santa Rosa itself. It does not establish where residents shopped, where animals physically boarded trains, or how much employment the arrangement displaced.

## Rock Island's own instructions confirm the car-order mechanism

A second original, the *Yard Clerical Manual*, explains the procedure at **section 14-2 / PDF 145**: yard clerks receive requests for freight cars from agents, car distributors and customers, and move available equipment as instructed. Requests coming from other sources are to be referred to the agent. Thus requesting equipment was a normal part of freight operations.

At **section 9-2 / PDF 97**, it explains that station lists and operating publications help staff route and group cars, and describes car orders as instructions for distributing empty equipment. At **sections 26-1 and 26-2 / PDF 232-233**, it discusses livestock shipments and recording loading time on waybills. The latter page bears revision date **December 1, 1971**; this does not date every page of the manual.

- [Online manual](https://www.rockislandline.org/_files/ugd/04ced6_4bc75f86e3c4458ba3de7129932a587a.pdf).
- [Held manual](../sources/01_railroad/local_functions_2026-09-20/ri_yard_clerks_manual.pdf), 313 pages, SHA-256 `cea4b9f458deed952ff726c80e7cdc352d6ac5a763e0f9018dcaca8c4f0ae0c3`.
- The scan metadata describes a 1970s manual. The cover carries no exact issue date; only the cited page's revision date has been verified.
- Relevant complete pages were read visually. The entire manual has not been transcribed or exhaustively searched; it also lacks embedded text.

These instructions support the general mechanism on the actual carrier, in the relevant later era. They do not establish that Cuervo remained open for livestock or that a local agent had authority to order stock cars for it in any particular year.

## More precise archival search targets

**Later September 20 search:** [new findings and acquisition log](cuervo_freight_records_search_2026-09-20.md) add RITS car-movement microfiche and a 1958 freight-directory lead. OU's current catalog says both Rock Island collections are temporarily unavailable for preservation work begun May 18, 2026. The targets below remain unacquired; no post-1958 Cuervo cattle transaction was found in the follow-up.

The new codes make a request materially more specific. Ask for **Cuervo, NM; revenue/audit 8355; car-accounting 16178; SPLC 774115; billing station Tucumcari 8310**, specifying that these identifiers are documented in 1974 and may differ in earlier years.

The goal is a dated waybill, freight-car order, station traffic return, or commodity report that names cattle and identifies the actual loading origin. A bill issued at Tucumcari could concern a shipment loaded elsewhere; both fields must be checked. Search a Cuervo record under Tucumcari's billing files as well as under Cuervo itself.

Specific OU inventory targets, all **unacquired records**:

1. **Box 157: Station Master, ledger, 1979** (finding aid PDF 30). Potential later comparison with the acquired 1974 station book; fields and Cuervo coverage unconfirmed.
2. **Box 480: mixed materials including waybill reports, 1966-1984** (finding aid PDF 44). Geographic and commodity coverage unspecified. This is a lead, not a cataloged Cuervo waybill.
3. **Box 8, folder 1: Ford, Bacon & Davis appraisal of Rock Island properties between Santa Rosa and St. Louis, 1977-1980** (finding aid PDF 7). The route includes Cuervo; station-level facility or traffic detail is unconfirmed.
4. **Boxes 371-389: ICC Finance Docket 28799, St. Louis Southwestern purchase of part of Rock Island, 1979** (finding aid PDF 37). Potential route inventory/traffic exhibits. No station figures acquired.

The already identified New Mexico State Archives Rock Island annual reports, 1950-1969, remain a separate potential source for earlier station returns. Their level of detail is unconfirmed. No email was sent during this search.

## Research conclusion

The working statement can now be more precise:

> Cuervo lost its advertised passenger stop within 1958, but remained separately identified in Rock Island's December 1974 freight-accounting reference, without an agent and with billing assigned to Tucumcari. The last local cattle shipment and the period when livestock loading remained available are still unknown.

This supports investigating continued freight access independently of passenger service. It does not establish uninterrupted service from 1958 to 1974, functioning pens in 1974, actual cattle-car volume, or the cause of Cuervo's decline.

The original downloads and checksums are recorded in acquisition manifests 9-12 in the dated railroad source folder. New facts and the bounded negative searches are retained here so future work can pursue the remaining gaps without repeating the same searches.
