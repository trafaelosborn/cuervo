## Later comparison, after the initial records are understood

- **Volume 207:** Bond & Wiest cash book, December 1930-September 1933.
- **Volume 208:** Bond & Wiest cash book, August 1941-November 1943.
- **Volume 216:** Bond & Wiest journal, January 4, 1945-October 30, 1954.

These may reveal changing customers, purchases, expenses, employees or transfers, depending on their contents. Establish comparable accounting categories before comparing numbers across ledgers, cash books and journals. Neither a catalog endpoint nor a final recorded transaction alone establishes closure.

## Capture with every record

- Collection, container and exact title; covers, title pages, account index, inserts and attachments.
- Full, legible page images in original order, including page numbers and both sides where written on.
- Customer names and recorded addresses; goods, quantities, prices, credit, settlements, freight and destination references.
- Notes distinguishing branch accounts from company totals, and cash movement from sales or profit.

Keep original images. Transcription, name matching, totals and comparison can be done afterward.

## Remaining work held elsewhere

1. **Town formation and land:** town-company book at the State Archives; original town plat, deeds and early assessments through Archives and Guadalupe County. Establish promoters, land ownership, actual dates and preexisting settlement.
2. **Local railway functions:** station creation, physical facilities, payroll, freight returns and shipment records. Passenger service dates do not answer local freight or employment questions.
3. **School decisions:** district boundaries, annual enrollment, finance, transport and actual consolidation/closure minutes. Separate pupils leaving a school from households leaving the area.
4. **Later commercial changes and ranch continuity:** explicit sale/closure notices, ownership and parcel histories, ranch employment, and transactions with Santa Rosa, Tucumcari and other places. These test changes in where people earned and spent money.

The central evidence chain remains: **who expected a business to work here; who actually sustained it; and what changed in those relationships.**
