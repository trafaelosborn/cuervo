# Home air conditioning and the Cuervo argument

**Research completed September 24, 2026. Interpretation updated September 25.**

## Current assessment

Home cooling improved the possibilities for comfortable residence, but offers little explanation for Cuervo's loss of services. Room units provided an alternative to central systems. Modest savings on cooling elsewhere would offer little reason to bear the expense and disruption of moving an established household. No evidence in the sweep connects a cooling advantage to moves to Santa Rosa. This assessment assigns the explanation limited weight; it does not estimate a zero total effect.

The [full research memorandum](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/research_evidence/home_cooling_2026-09-24.md) supersedes the earlier limited literature check. It adds visually checked census tables and definitions, a historical utility source, reproducible NOAA temperature calculations, and an expanded literature assessment with reading coverage recorded.

## What changed

- **Local uptake is documented at two geographic levels.** Guadalupe County reported AC in about 3.0% of occupied housing in 1960, about 29.0% of year-round housing in the 1970 AC distribution, and 45.2% of year-round housing in 1980. The occupied/year-round denominator change prevents treating this as a harmonized household series.
- **Santa Rosa has a direct 1980 observation:** 66.1% of 985 year-round units. Its population fell from 2,485 in 1970 to 2,469 in 1980, then to 2,263 in 1990. Uptake and population stagnation coexisted. This does not identify the no-cooling counterfactual.
- **The census excluded evaporative coolers in all three years.** Reported AC does not measure every form of cooling. Biddle also notes possible respondent misclassification in 1960.
- **Room units matter to the proposed mechanism.** The 1960 county table reports 42 occupied housing units with room equipment and none with central air. This establishes regional use, not a Cuervo adoption rate. Exact equality of installation costs is unnecessary to judge modest savings a weak motive for relocation. The earlier assessment gave that unmeasured comparison too much weight.
- **The climate comparison has a concrete limit.** NOAA's Cuervo inventory has precipitation and snow observations but no maximum/minimum temperature series. Santa Rosa has historical temperature data. The sweep does not assign those readings to Cuervo.

## Manuscript decision

Draft 6 gives this explanation proportionate treatment in one paragraph: the room-unit alternative, the expense and disruption of moving, and the absence of evidence that cooling drove relocation to Santa Rosa. Its 1980 uptake and population figures remain contextual evidence. The note retains the census definition and distinguishes the book's inference from an estimated causal effect. The accessibility revision and institutional Meridian voice remain in place.

No extra archival collection is required. The inability to prove a zero effect does not justify another research requirement. Revisit the conclusion only if substantive evidence of a local cooling disadvantage linked to residential decisions emerges.

The preceding assessments, manuscript and proof are preserved in [draft 5's archive](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/manuscript/revisions/chapter-01-draft-05). Automotive cooling remains a separate lead in draft 3's archive; it is not the answer to the reader's household question.
