# Chapter 7: source coverage and interpretation

**September 28, 2026. Draft 1.**

Chapter 7 synthesizes the preceding six chapters and the September 24 mechanism comparison. It does not open a new general collection program. Historical observations are carried forward with their existing source limits. Repeated appearances of a source are not independent corroboration.

These are primary theoretical texts. The continuation-versus-entry discussion is an explicit analytical illustration. It neither introduces invented Cuervo revenue numbers nor claims an estimated irreversible-investment model. It distinguishes avoidable continuing costs, entry costs and the opportunity value of assets.

## Historical evidence carried forward

- Grubbs's Cuervo accounts and 1958 observation: Chapter 3, notes 1 and 8. Twelve positive annual net-profit entries in 1904-1915 are not an audited record of all subsequent years.
- Rosa correspondence: Chapter 3, note 19; not Santa Rosa or Cuervo. Original letters unread; reproduced passages used.
- Teacher, doctor, publisher and garage: Chapter 4, notes 1, 9-10, 13 and 15. Announcement, proposal, financial withdrawal and reported reopening retain separate statuses.
- School-related moves: Chapter 4, notes 3 and 5. The positive historical relationship is observed; a later reverse cascade is not measured.
- Agricultural inventories and real cattle prices: Chapter 3, notes 20-29. County inventories, state prices and precinct population remain separate geographic quantities.
- El Rancho Milling: Chapter 3, note 22 and Chapter 6. The 1947 network includes a Cuervo dealer; it does not demonstrate local displacement.
- Timetable, retail and railway billing: Chapter 6, notes 2-5 and 17. Convenience differs from distance, sales share does not identify customers, billing codes do not count shipments.
- Households and ages: Chapter 5, notes 1-13. Precinct totals include countryside; head groups are not new dwellings. Corrected 1950 median remains 25.5, although only the 1940 median is used in the main text.
- Chavez and Higgins histories: selected retrospective cases from Chapter 5; neither representative migration evidence nor proof of uninterrupted residence.
- Santa Rosa/Los Lunas: Chapter 5, notes 20-22, distinguish household population, group quarters and municipal footprint.
- Road and rail dates: Chapter 6, notes 8, 12-17 and 21. 1952 relocation, 1958 timetable bracket and 1963-1965 project are distinct.
- Home cooling: September 24 sweep and Chapter 1. Room units and moving costs weaken the relocation hypothesis. Census adoption excludes evaporative cooling; Santa Rosa observation is context, not an effect estimate.

## Claims deliberately kept bounded

The chapter explains an observed contraction and reorganization of local functions. The dominant causal shares and an uninterrupted later cascade remain unestablished. It does not claim that ranch production disappeared, that Santa Rosa absorbed the entire community, or that the interstate initiated all contraction. Persistence and reopening remain counterevidence to inevitability.

The strongest causal material concerns specific early organizational decisions and school-related moves. Wider transport concentration and feedback provide a conditional synthesis. This distinction permits a substantive explanation without claiming more identification than the records provide. The book can proceed to Chapter 8 without exhaustive reconstruction of every business or household.
