# Cuervo research: verified catalog leads and pull list

Researched September 16, 2026, for Taylor Osborn.

## What this research establishes

The catalogs contain substantial, specific leads for Cuervo's town company, businesses, schools, voting, telephone service, and post office. These support a comparison of Cuervo and Santa Rosa before, during, and after highway changes.

**Evidence level:** The references below were verified in repository finding aids, item records, or official library guides. Unless explicitly described as digitized, the underlying historical documents have not been inspected. A catalog title identifies something to request; it does not establish what the document will prove. Research uses suggested below are hypotheses.

The State Archives' statement that county holdings *typically* end in 1912 has an important exception: its [Guadalupe County collection, 1974-020](https://nmarchives.unm.edu/repositories/32/resources/5903), spans 1892–1956, with bulk dates 1893–1923. Its description lists clerk records through 1956, assessor records through 1943, commissioners' records through 1952, and school records through 1942. The finding aid also says most statehood records remain with the county. Both repositories remain relevant; this does not establish complete coverage for those spans.

### UNM William J. Lucas Route 66 Photographs

[Collection PICT-986-012](https://nmarchives.unm.edu/repositories/22/resources/2746): photographs dated 1967–1986, including Cuervo and Santa Rosa. This provides a possible visual comparison within one photographer's work.

An actual digitized item is available now: [Cuervo, New Mexico, March 1986](https://econtent.unm.edu/digital/collection/wjlr66/id/9/rec/1), William J. Lucas, identifier **986-012-0009.tif**. The date is verified from item metadata; it cannot alone establish when a depicted building became vacant or damaged.

The collection finding aid and digital item contain differing rights statements. Ask UNM to clarify reuse terms before publication.

## How these sources test the research question

1. **Town formation and ownership:** town-company book, early assessments, and property records.
2. **Economic trajectory before I-40:** Bond & Wiest accounts, inventories, and regional newspapers. Establish comparable measures before asserting a decline date.
3. **Institutional continuity:** school minutes and petitions, postal correspondence, telephone-company records. Record actual decision dates separately from later recollections.
4. **Highway mechanism and timing:** NMDOT project records plus State Archives highway inventories; distinguish the 1952 Route 66 relocation from the 1960s interstate project.
5. **Comparative outcome:** use matched Santa Rosa and Cuervo population/geographic definitions, business measures, and service dates. Santa Rosa's growth or institutional advantages should be demonstrated with comparable evidence, not assumed to be explained by any single source above.

The immediate next step is a specific Archives reproduction/appointment inquiry using the cited units, followed by separate inquiries to UNM, the Palace photo archives, and NMSU. None of those follow-up emails was sent during this catalog research.
