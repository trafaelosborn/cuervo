**Producer:** Space Ranger Press

## Governing Principles

- **The 50/50 discipline.** Essay and plates are peers, not master/servant. The essay must stand as an essay — an argument, not inflated captions. The plates must function as evidence, not decoration. If either could be removed without loss, the balance has failed.
- **Non-goal: ruin-porn.** The book is not an elegy for a pretty dead town. Atmosphere without argument is the failure mode. Every plate should earn its place against the thesis.
- **Gates, not dates.** Each phase has an explicit exit condition. Do not begin a phase until the prior gate is cleared. This is the only defense a no-deadline project has against entropy.
- **Standalone, but serves the Society.** The book must work fully on its own terms AND be deployable as the org's calling card. The Society's undefined operational state must never block the book.
- **Epistemic hygiene.** Every date, name, and causal claim the essay asserts must trace to a verifiable source. See the register below.

## Thesis (REVISED against evidence — August 2026)

### What the evidence killed

The original three-regime thesis — rail, then Route 66, then "bypassed and strangled by I-40 (~1970)" — does not survive its own data.

- **The date was wrong.** I-40 here was built 1963–65, project I-040-5(18)1283, not ~1970.
- **"Bypassed" is false.** Cuervo retains Exit 291, and 11,000–20,000 vehicles a day passed it through the 1990s.
- **There was never a Route 66 boom to lose.** Cuervo declined in *every* decade from 1910 to 1940. Its peak is the first census in which it appears.
- **The interstate cannot be the cause.** By 1940 Cuervo was down 65% from 1910 — twenty-three years before construction began. And every settlement on this corridor has an interchange; two thrived and three died.

### What replaced it

**Cuervo was a friction town. It existed because Santa Rosa was just far enough away, and it died as that distance got cheaper.**

Twenty miles is a hard day's round trip by wagon and seventeen minutes at interstate speed. Cuervo's entire reason for existing was the cost of covering that gap. Every improvement to the road — the 1932 realignment, the 1935–36 paving, the 1963–65 interstate — eroded the condition that justified the town, and transferred its functions to the county seat twenty miles west.

The evidence for this is a divergence, not a decline. In the 1930s, the paving decade, Cuervo fell 34.5% while Santa Rosa town grew 105% and Guadalupe County grew 23%. Cuervo was not emptying with its region. It was emptying *into* Santa Rosa.

*The mechanism is ordinary economic geography and the book should say so plainly — see `research_evidence/analytical_frame.md`. The book's contribution is not the theory, which is a century old. It is one settlement documented at a resolution the literature has never reached.*

### What is still open

- The 1910–1930 decline shows no divergence and is probably the dryland homestead bust, not absorption. Two mechanisms in sequence, and they need separating.
- Whether rail-era Cuervo was already inside Santa Rosa's reach. Testable against the 1926 timetable already held: did the schedule permit a day return?

**Standing guard:** economic determinism reads as fate, which is the elegy this project forbids in another costume. The pressure was structural; the decisions had names, dates, and signatures. Keep both.

## Epistemic Register

Kept live. Move items up as they're verified. The essay may only assert from the FACTS column.

### Established (confident)
- Cuervo is a ghost town in Guadalupe County, NM, on old Route 66.
- Santa Rosa is the Guadalupe County seat; courthouse at 1448 Historic Route 66.
- Cuervo and Santa Rosa are ~20 mi apart on I-40 (records + reshoot = one field day).
- **I-40 construction here: project I-040-5(18)1283, MP 282.7 to ~290.7, initiated 1963, completed 1965** (NMSHTD records quoted in NTSB factual report, held locally).
- **Traffic at the Cuervo interchange: 11,415–20,051 AADT, 1993–2001**, 32–50% commercial. The town was not starved of traffic.
- **Population series 1910–1950, verified figure by figure off page renders** — Cuervo precinct 545 / 438 / 287 / 188 / 210. See `research_evidence/population_series_verified.md`.
- **Santa Rosa comparison:** town 1,003 (1920) / 1,127 (1930) / 2,310 (1940) / 2,199 (1950). The 1930s divergence is the central fact of the argument.
- **NRHP status: the town is not listed.** Two road segments are — Cuervo→SR 156 listed 22 Nov 1993, Montoya→Cuervo listed 19 Nov 1997 (no. 97001395). Both nominations held locally.
- **Post office discontinued 10 September 2011**; ZIP 88417 retained, administration to Newkirk. Not a legal abandonment.
- Peak population is the **first** census in which Cuervo appears (1910). There is no later peak to find.

## Phase 2 — Photographic Completion

**Objective:** Close the gap between existing shots and what the (now-locked) argument demands.

- [ ] Audit existing plates against the essay skeleton — which arguments lack an image?
- [ ] **Santa Rosa is now a photographic subject, not a logistics stop.** Under the revised thesis the book cannot be photographs of Cuervo alone. What absorbed Cuervo's functions is evidence: the working school, the working grocery, the occupied motels, and the courthouse at 1448 Historic Route 66 — the county seat's civic function sitting on the same road that drained Cuervo.
- [ ] **The corridor is a subject.** Twenty miles at seventy miles an hour is seventeen minutes, and that number is the argument. Shoot it as a timed sequence.
- [ ] Consider Newkirk and Montoya — the two other casualties on the transect. Even a single frame each converts the corridor from assertion into evidence.
- [ ] Build targeted reshoot list (specific structures, angles, drone verticals)
- [ ] Consider season/light for reshoots — **existing take is harsh winter midday with sun in frame; that is the hardest possible light for architectural documentation**
- [ ] **Fix capture metadata before shooting.** All 73 existing stills have GPS but `datetime_original` stripped. Section F of the research scaffold requires coordinates, azimuth, lens, and date per plate; the existing take satisfies one of four.
- [ ] **Resolve permissions before interiors.** Several existing frames are inside structures on land whose owners of record are not yet established. This is blocked behind the assessor work in Phase 1.
- [ ] Shoot the drone verticals. The plan specifies a DJI Air 3; every existing frame is iPhone. The severance and alignment geometry is the one thing only an aerial can carry.
- [ ] **Reshoot expedition** — combine with Guadalupe County records run (one overland day; DJI Air 3 + ground)
- [ ] Edit and select final plate set
- [ ] Classify each plate by register — documentary vs. evocative
- [ ] Sequence plates and map their interleave with the essay
