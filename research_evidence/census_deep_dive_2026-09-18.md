## 1. What this changes

**Santa Rosa's later population growth needs a substantial qualification: its household population was almost unchanged between 1990 and 2020.** The total rose from 2,263 to 2,850, but the number of people living in households rose from 2,240 to 2,261. Group quarters accounted for 566 of the 587 additional residents, or 96.4% of the net increase. In 2020, the census classified 585 Santa Rosa residents in correctional facilities for adults. [S4, S7]

That is a major distinction for the book. Santa Rosa retained a substantial residential community and acquired an institutional population. Los Lunas experienced a much larger expansion of its household population. They should not be treated as interchangeable examples of a town growing beside an interstate.

The other major finding concerns Cuervo itself. The 1950 schedules contain **85 children under 18 among 210 named residents**, and the median recorded age is **25**. Fourteen residents were 65 or older. The surviving community included many children and younger adults; an image of an almost exclusively elderly remnant does not fit this census. [S1]

This pass adds four things to the earlier research:

- A complete, auditable age reading of Cuervo's nine retained 1950 population sheets.
- Published population comparisons through 2020, separating households from group quarters where available.
- Housing, household size, age, and municipal land-area checks that change how the growth curves should be read.
- A clearer distinction between documented population patterns and the transportation, migration, or economic mechanisms that might explain them.

All figures below describe the named reporting geography at each census date. Municipal boundaries changed. These are not estimates for an unchanging footprint.

## 2. Santa Rosa's growth includes a large institutional component

The Census Bureau divides people between **households** and **group quarters**. A household consists of people occupying a housing unit; it can be one person, a family, or unrelated people. Group quarters include prisons, nursing facilities, dormitories, and other communal facilities. Both categories contain residents. The distinction matters because they represent different forms of residential and economic demand.

| Census year | Total residents | People in households | People in group quarters | Households |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1980 | 2,469 | 2,467 | 2 | 811 |
| 1990 | 2,263 | 2,240 | 23 | 810 |
| 2000 | 2,744 | 2,241 | 503 | 898 |
| 2010 | 2,848 | 2,280 | 568 | 981 |
| 2020 | 2,850 | 2,261 | 589 | 980 |

Sources: 1980 Table 14; 1990 Tables 1, 2 and 75; 2000 Tables 9 and 10; 2010 Demographic Profile; 2020 DHC. The 1980 household population is total population minus group quarters. [S3-S7]

![Santa Rosa population by living arrangement, 1980-2020](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/research_evidence/census_deep_dive_2026-09-18/figures/santa_rosa_households_group_quarters.png)

### The sharp break is in the 1990s

Between 1990 and 2000, Santa Rosa gained **481 total residents**. Its household population increased by **one**, while its group-quarters population increased by **480**. The striking upward segment in the total-population curve therefore does not describe a comparable influx of people into homes.

The timing fits a specific institution. The New Mexico Corrections Department's 2012-13 annual report records the **Guadalupe County Correctional Facility opening in 1999**, in Santa Rosa. Its current facility history says it was **built in 1997**; construction and opening are different dates. The 2000 census reports 497 institutionalized residents in the city, and the 2020 DHC identifies 585 residents in adult correctional facilities. Together, these sources strongly support the prison as the explanation for the late-1990s jump. [S5, S7, S8]

Group quarters should not automatically be renamed "prison population." The tables also contain other kinds of facilities. The exact accounting claim is that **96.4% of Santa Rosa's net 1990-2020 population increase occurred in group quarters**. The correctional-facility cells and the opening chronology identify the dominant institutional explanation.

### Stable households can still reflect economic resilience

Removing the group-quarters component does not measure what Santa Rosa would have been without the prison. Employment, procurement, and public revenue associated with the institution could help support households. We have not estimated those effects.

The safer conclusion is that Santa Rosa maintained roughly 2,200-2,300 people in households from 1990 through 2020, while adding a major institutional population. Its household population in 2020 was **8.4% below 1980's 2,467**. The city is a case of residential persistence through regional decline, with an institutional addition; its population counts alone do not establish broad prosperity or rapid residential expansion.

That changes the comparison behind "I-40 went through Santa Rosa too, and it grew." Santa Rosa's 1960s growth remains real in the published counts. Its much later growth has a different demographic explanation. The decades should be analyzed separately.

## 3. Los Lunas remains a striking contrast

The household comparison survives the same group-quarters adjustment.

| Census year | Santa Rosa household population | Los Lunas household population |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1980 | 2,467 | 3,158 |
| 1990 | 2,240 | 5,936 |
| 2000 | 2,241 | 9,920 |
| 2010 | 2,280 | 14,688 |
| 2020 | 2,261 | 16,994 |

For Los Lunas, 1980 and 1990 household population is the published total minus group quarters. Later values are direct household-population cells. [S3-S7]

![Santa Rosa and Los Lunas household populations on the same scale](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/research_evidence/census_deep_dive_2026-09-18/figures/household_population_comparison.png)

From 1990 to 2020:

- **Santa Rosa:** household population increased **0.9%**, from 2,240 to 2,261; households increased from 810 to 980.
- **Los Lunas:** household population increased **186.3%**, from 5,936 to 16,994; households increased from 2,059 to 6,393.

Los Lunas also has institutional populations, and the municipal village is smaller than the surrounding Los Lunas census county division. In 2020, the village had 248 people in group quarters; the larger division had 1,849. Subtracting the same geographic unit's group quarters is essential. An institution with a Los Lunas mailing address cannot simply be assigned to the village count. [S7]

### What the contrast says about the working model

These observations are consistent with the idea that a community near a large metropolitan labor and housing market can grow in ways unavailable to a small settlement near a much smaller regional center. They do not, by themselves, establish that commuting to Albuquerque caused the Los Lunas growth, or identify a population threshold at which a larger town starts benefiting its neighbors.

Testing the proposed mechanism requires evidence about **where residents worked, where new housing appeared, travel times, municipal annexations, and local employment**. A similar-looking curve is useful for selecting a model to investigate. Several mechanisms can generate the same curve.

There are now three distinct outcomes to explain: Cuervo's long decline, Santa Rosa's ability to sustain a household community, and Los Lunas's much larger residential expansion. The evidence does not require one threshold rule to explain all three.

## 4. The longer regional timeline

| Census year | Santa Rosa | Los Lunas | Guadalupe County | Tucumcari | Vaughn |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1970 | 2,485 | 973 | 4,969 | 7,189 | 867 |
| 1980 | 2,469 | 3,525 | 4,496 | 6,765 | 737 |
| 1990 | 2,263 | 6,013 | 4,156 | 6,831 | 633 |
| 2000 | 2,744 | 10,034 | 4,680 | 5,989 | 539 |
| 2010 | 2,848 | 14,835 | 4,687 | 5,363 | 446 |
| 2020 | 2,850 | 17,242 | 4,452 | 5,278 | 286 |

City/town/village counts except the county column. Historical entries come from the 1990 retrospective tables and the 2000 publication; modern entries come from the official bulk files. [S4-S7]

### Four periods worth keeping separate

**1950-1970: county decline, modest Santa Rosa growth.** Santa Rosa went from 2,199 in 1950 to 2,220 in 1960 and 2,485 in 1970. Guadalupe County went from 6,772 to 5,610 to 4,969. Santa Rosa's 1960-70 gain was 265 people, or 11.9%; the county outside the municipality fell from 3,390 to 2,484, or 26.7%. These are descriptive subtractions, not tracked moves. [S2]

**1970-1990: Santa Rosa also loses population.** It was nearly flat in the 1970s and fell 8.3% in the 1980s, from 2,469 to 2,263. Its household population fell by 227 during that decade. Meanwhile Los Lunas rose from 973 in 1970 to 6,013 in 1990. A smooth story of uninterrupted Santa Rosa growth misses this reversal.

**1990-2000: institutional growth changes the headline.** Guadalupe County's total increased from 4,156 to 4,680, but its household population increased only from 4,133 to 4,155. Group quarters account for 502 of the county's 524 additional residents. Santa Rosa's new institutional population therefore changes the county story as well as the city story.

**2000-2020: household population remains concentrated in Santa Rosa while the rest of the county contracts.** Santa Rosa's household population rose from 2,241 to 2,261; the county's fell from 4,155 to 3,855. Subtracting the city leaves **1,914 to 1,594** people in households elsewhere in the county, a decline of **16.7%**. That pattern fits increasing concentration of the remaining residential population at the county seat. It does not identify where the departing residents went.

### The larger comparison towns matter

Between 1970 and 2020, Tucumcari's published population fell **26.6%**, and Vaughn's fell **67.0%**. A town larger than Cuervo could still lose residents over a long period. Size might help sustain particular services without guaranteeing growth.

Vaughn's 2020 median age was **50.7**, compared with **39.6** in Santa Rosa and **37.0** in Los Lunas. This is evidence of different present-day age structures, not proof of which process originally caused decline. Institutional populations also affect municipal age measures. [S7]

### A correction to our previous reading

The original 1970 table, re-read at higher resolution, prints **2,485** for Santa Rosa, corroborated by the 1980 and 1990 retrospective tables. Our September 17 analysis used **2,483**. The corrected 1960-70 increase is **265 / 11.9%**, and the corrected county remainder in 1970 is **2,484**. This small transcription correction does not change the interpretation, but it should propagate to future tables and prose. [S2, S4]

## 5. More households can coexist with a stable population

Santa Rosa illustrates why population, household count, and housing stock should be treated as different measures.

| Measure | 1990 | 2000 | 2010 | 2020 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| People in households | 2,240 | 2,241 | 2,280 | 2,261 |
| Occupied housing units / households | 810 | 898 | 981 | 980 |
| People per household, calculated | 2.77 | 2.50 | 2.32 | 2.31 |
| Total housing units | 988 | 1,024 | 1,178 | 1,230 |
| Vacant housing units | 178 | 126 | 197 | 250 |
| Vacant share of all units | 18.0% | 12.3% | 16.7% | 20.3% |

Average household size is household population divided by households, rounded to two decimals. It excludes group quarters. Vacant share here is all vacant units divided by all housing units; it is not the Census Bureau's narrower homeowner or rental vacancy rate. [S4-S7]

From 1990 to 2020, Santa Rosa added **170 households** while adding only **21 household residents**. People lived in smaller households. The number of one-person households rose from **180 to 354**; the number of family households fell from **614 to 551**. Those are useful measures of changing community composition even when the total population looks stable.

For 2020, compare the share of all housing units classified as vacant:

| Reporting unit | Housing units | Vacant units | Vacant share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Santa Rosa | 1,230 | 250 | 20.3% |
| Los Lunas | 6,705 | 312 | 4.7% |
| Tucumcari | 2,895 | 591 | 20.4% |
| Vaughn | 170 | 36 | 21.2% |
| Guadalupe County | 2,187 | 528 | 24.1% |

The contrast adds evidence of different housing conditions. It does not establish prices, physical condition, market availability, or abandonment. Vacant units can include seasonal homes, units for sale or rent, and other temporarily unoccupied housing. A housing unit also is not necessarily a freestanding house. [S7]

Santa Rosa's 2010 profile, for example, separately identifies 21 seasonally or occasionally used vacant units and 104 in the "other vacant" category, out of 197 vacant units overall. Neither category alone establishes dereliction. The remaining categories account for the rest. [S6]

The new transcription reads the recorded age of every person in the previously counted 1950 Cuervo district, ED 10-6. It includes sheets 1-8 and sheet 71, totaling **210 named people**. This covers the precinct/district population, including surrounding rural territory; it is not a count restricted to the surviving buildings at Cuervo. [S1]

|---|---:|---:|
| 0-4 | 22 | 10.5% |
| 5-17 | 63 | 30.0% |
| 18-34 | 51 | 24.3% |
| 35-49 | 30 | 14.3% |
| 50-64 | 30 | 14.3% |
| 65 and older | 14 | 6.7% |

Percentages may not sum exactly to 100 because of rounding. Median recorded age: **25 years**. Under 18: **85 / 40.5%**. [S1]

![Cuervo's 1950 age distribution from a complete manual reading](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/research_evidence/census_deep_dive_2026-09-18/figures/cuervo_1950_age_profile.png)

Guadalupe County's published 1950 median age was **21.0**, and **5.2%** of residents were 65 or older. Cuervo was somewhat older on these measures than its county, but still had a substantial child population. The comparison is approximate: our median comes from completed integer ages in the manuscripts, while the published county median follows the Bureau's tabulation method. [S2]

### What this does to the war hypothesis

It supports a specific statement: **a community containing many children and working-age adults remained in Cuervo after the war and before the 1952 road relocation**. Combined with the earlier occupation readings, this adds substance to the finding that Cuervo still functioned as a working community in 1950.

It does not settle the proposition that Cuervo was older than Santa Rosa before the war and therefore sent fewer men into military service. We still need the **1940 age and sex distributions**, along with service and migration evidence. Children born during the 1940s, returning veterans, deaths, and migration all intervene between the two census dates. A 1950 age profile cannot be projected backward as though those events did not happen.

The increase from 188 in 1940 to 210 in 1950 is **22 people / 11.7%**. A small number of household arrivals, births, or departures can substantially change such a small population. Census endpoints cannot locate the increase within the decade or attribute it to wartime freight and beef demand.

### Transcription quality and unresolved details

The sheet counts reconcile to 210. The original cover separately carries a field total of 210 and a Washington-office total of 211, with one ICR noted; that administrative discrepancy remains unresolved. The published precinct figure is 210, matching the visible named rows.

Infants recorded by birth month are coded age zero. Several difficult ages were rechecked in enlarged crops. A record whose sex column says "M" while the relationship says "wife" is retained as written in the transcription; this report does not use it to make a sex-ratio argument. The age reading is unaffected. One crossed-through and rewritten name on sheet 7 is retained as one row, consistent with the existing person inventory.

The saved transcription is a research reading, not a replacement for the manuscripts. It preserves sheet order and reading notes so another reader can audit the results.

## 7. Geography is part of the explanation

The original Cuervo observations are election-precinct counts. From 1960 onward, the published county-subdivision tables use **census county divisions**, a different statistical geography. A large Santa Rosa division cannot be substituted for Cuervo precinct 6.

Neither the 2010 nor the 2020 place-level files inspected here contain a named Cuervo place. That does **not** mean the census counted zero people at Cuervo. It means a separate published place total is unavailable in those files. A modern settlement count would require an explicit study boundary and appropriate census blocks. A comparison with 1950 would additionally require the historical district boundary. Newkirk's modern census-designated place is also not a substitute for Cuervo or for the much larger old Newkirk precinct.

Municipal comparisons have their own boundary issue:

| Land area, square miles | 1990 | 2010 | 2020 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Santa Rosa city | 2.8 | 4.97 | 5.00 |
| Los Lunas village | 6.1 | 14.63 | 18.73 |

The 1990 figures are rounded as printed; modern areas are calculated from the official square-meter geographic headers. The 1990 publication also flags earlier annexations and division changes. Area changes may reflect annexation and geographic measurement updates. They do not tell us how many people were added by a boundary change. [S4, S6, S7]

Los Lunas's population increase is an increase in the population of the village as reported at successive dates. It cannot all be attributed to births or people moving into an unchanged village boundary. Conversely, annexing undeveloped land would not itself explain a large added population. Boundary histories and block-level overlays are needed to separate those processes.

The 2020 DHC also uses disclosure-avoidance procedures to protect respondents. Small differences and very fine age-by-facility cells should not be treated as exact historical events. Santa Rosa's difference of **two total residents** between 2010 and 2020 is best described as essentially flat. The large contrast between its household trend and Los Lunas's is much more informative. [S7]

## 8. The argument the census can presently support

The revised working interpretation is:

> Cuervo's decline preceded the interstate, but a substantial working and family community remained in 1950. Santa Rosa retained a larger residential base through decades of regional contraction and later gained a major correctional population. Los Lunas underwent a much larger expansion of households within a growing municipal footprint. These outcomes point toward different regional roles, access to markets and jobs, and institutional support; population curves alone do not identify the causal contribution of each.

This preserves the useful part of the transportation argument while making it more precise. Transportation can change which places supply services, where people can work while living elsewhere, and where employers or institutions locate. The census supplies outcomes to explain. It does not directly observe shopping trips, freight revenue, customer loss, commuting destinations, or decisions to close a school or discontinue regular Mass.

The question for Santa Rosa becomes **how it sustained a household population near 2,200 despite a shrinking hinterland**, and what role the prison and other institutions played. The question for Los Lunas becomes **how many households and jobs were added through metropolitan integration, local employment, and territorial expansion**. The question for Cuervo remains **which losses in work, services, and access made continued residence harder, and when**.

### The next three census investigations

1. **Test the prewar age hypothesis.** Transcribe Cuervo's 1940 ages and a clearly defined Santa Rosa comparison population, using matching age groups. That would let us evaluate the claimed prewar age difference directly.
2. **Build a geographic bridge beyond 1950.** Reconstruct Cuervo's 1950 district, define a modern settlement footprint, and extract block totals for appropriate later censuses. Report settlement and district results separately.
3. **Link households across 1940 and 1950.** Match names, ages, relationships, and occupations with uncertainty retained. Identify observed continuities and departures before seeking destinations. Disappearance from one district does not establish death or movement to Santa Rosa.

1950 remains the latest publicly released individual population census. The National Archives schedules the 1960 household records for **April 2032**. Published aggregate statistics remain available for intervening decades. This investigation therefore uses individual schedules for 1950 and published tables for later years. [S9]

### Reproduction and scope of the work

The [bulk-file parser](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/research_evidence/census_deep_dive_2026-09-18/extract_modern.py) follows the official layouts and retains geographic identifiers and raw values. The [manual published readings](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/research_evidence/census_deep_dive_2026-09-18/manual_published_readings.json) preserve source locators. The [analysis script](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/research_evidence/census_deep_dive_2026-09-18/build_analysis.py) produces [combined observations](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/research_evidence/census_deep_dive_2026-09-18/population_housing_observations.json), [calculations](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/research_evidence/census_deep_dive_2026-09-18/calculations.json), and the three figures.

Checks reconcile total population to household plus group-quarters population, housing units to occupied plus vacant units, modern totals to the geographic headers, and all Cuervo age bins to the 210-person inventory. These arithmetic checks cannot resolve census coverage error, historical boundary changes, or every possible manuscript-reading error.

This acquisition also saved a complete [1930 microfilm reel containing Guadalupe County EDs 10-1 through 10-27](https://archive.org/details/15thcensus1393unit). Its individual schedules have **not** been transcribed or analyzed in this report. The acquired 1940 schedules likewise remain the next direct test of the prewar age hypothesis. Income, commuting, business performance, and causal estimates are outside the completed census analysis presented here.
