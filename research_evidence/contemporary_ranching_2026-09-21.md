# Continued cattle ranching around Cuervo

*Verified September 21, 2026.*

## Finding

**Commercial cattle ranching around Cuervo is independently documented in 2026.** This confirms the activity described in the user's field observation, without identifying the families encountered on that visit.

### Recent recognition of an operating ranch

Sand County Foundation's **July 30, 2026** announcement names **Rancho Armendariz of Cuervo** among the New Mexico Leopold Conservation Award finalists. It describes a working cattle operation, rangeland restoration, and the family's A&G Family Meats business. The award organizer is a direct source for its finalist selection.

[Original announcement](https://sandcountyfoundation.org/news/2026/new-mexico-leopold-conservation-award-finalists-selected) · [Saved HTML](../sources/contemporary_ranching_2026-09-21/sand_county_award_2026.html).

### First-person geographic confirmation

In the New Mexico Beef Council's **January 20, 2026** *Behind the Burger* interview, **Jim Armendariz, at 6:10**, says his father bought a ranch outside Cuervo in **2020** and operates from there while using grazing leases in several other places. This is a first-person account of a physical operating base, beyond a mailing-address listing. The transcript was read; the audio was not independently checked.

[Episode and transcript](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2445388/episodes/18537637-building-a-family-ranch-with-science-grit-and-heart-with-the-armendariz-family) · [Saved HTML](../sources/contemporary_ranching_2026-09-21/armendariz_beef_council_interview.html).

### Supporting commercial evidence

The New Mexico Department of Agriculture's directory carries **A&G Family Meats**, describing its beef, pork and lamb business. This supports commercial activity but provides no Cuervo locality on the inspected page; geography comes from the sources above. The listing has no visible publication date.

[NMDA listing](https://members.elevatenmag.com/nmda_business/ag-family-meats/) · [Saved HTML](../sources/contemporary_ranching_2026-09-21/nmda_ag_family_meats.html).

## What this establishes, and what remains open

- A named cattle operation has a contemporary, explicitly documented connection to the Cuervo area.
- The family's operation includes scattered holdings. Its reported total acreage or cattle numbers must not be assigned wholesale to Cuervo or its historical census precinct.
- This is a current observation, not an annual record of uninterrupted ranching on the same properties throughout the twentieth century.
- It does not establish stable historical herd sizes, profitability, employment, resident population, local purchases or railway shipping.

**Implication for the project:** explaining the settlement's decline must accommodate the continued presence of a cattle economy in its surrounding area. The hypothesis that spending and services shifted elsewhere remains to be tested; ranching's survival alone does not prove that mechanism.
