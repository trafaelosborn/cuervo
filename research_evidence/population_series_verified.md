# Cuervo / Santa Rosa / Guadalupe County — Population Observations and Limits

*Revised September 18, 2026. [Earlier note preserved](deep_dive_2026-09-17/prior_working_notes/population_series_verified.md). The [September 18 census deep dive](census_deep_dive_2026-09-18.md) extends the comparison through 2020 and corrects the 1970 Santa Rosa transcription. These are published observations on the named reporting units, not a reconstructed constant-boundary series.*

| Geography | 1910 | 1920 | 1930 | 1940 | 1950 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Cuervo election precinct 6 | **545** | 438 | 287 | 188 | 210 |
| Santa Rosa election precinct 8 | 1,031 | 1,390 | 1,381 | 2,527 | 2,548 |
| Santa Rosa municipality | Not separately listed | **1,003 / 1,093 unresolved** | 1,127 | 2,310 | 2,199 |
| Guadalupe County | 10,927 | 8,015 | 7,027 | 8,646 | 6,772 |

Cuervo's 1910 figure is **545**, not the original scaffold's 645. The 1910 and 1920 precinct observations must not be treated as a census-sanctioned comparison: the 1920 table omits earlier precinct columns because of redistricting. County territory also changed.

### The 1920 Santa Rosa discrepancy

The 1920 volume, printed page 16 / PDF 18, visually appears to print **1,003**. Two 1930 tables, printed pages 734 and 739 / PDF 71 and 76, print its **1920** count as **1,093**. Rechecking the original scan did not reconcile the publications. The corresponding 1920–30 changes would be +12.4% or +3.1%; neither is settled pending a correction notice or further contemporary evidence.

## The 1930–1950 comparison

| Reporting unit | 1930–1940 | 1940–1950 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cuervo precinct | −99 / −34.5% | +22 / +11.7% |
| Santa Rosa municipality | +1,183 / +105.0% | −111 / −4.8% |
| Guadalupe County | +1,619 / +23.0% | −1,874 / −21.7% |

In 1930–40 the county outside Santa Rosa also grew, from 5,900 to 6,336. Cuervo declined amid wider growth. This does **not** show that departing Cuervo residents moved to Santa Rosa. Net changes combine births, deaths, arrivals, departures, and potentially boundary effects.

Cuervo's net loss of 99 is only 8.4% of Santa Rosa's gain of 1,183. That emphasizes the mismatch in scale; **it is not an upper bound on gross migration**, because opposing flows and natural increase can offset one another.

The 1940–50 pattern reverses: Cuervo grows while Santa Rosa and the county lose population. Continuous, measured transfer from one to the other does not fit these endpoints.

Santa Rosa's household population rose only 21 between 1990 and 2020. Group quarters accounted for 566 of its 587 additional total residents. The 2020 DHC classifies 585 city residents in adult correctional facilities; the NM Corrections Department dates the Guadalupe County facility opening to 1999. Los Lunas's household population rose 186.3% over 1990-2020. Municipal land areas changed substantially, so neither series is a fixed-footprint growth estimate.

The full Cuervo 1950 age transcription yields **210 people, median recorded age 25, 85 under 18, and 14 age 65 or older**. The subsequent [full-history extension](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/research_evidence/census_full_history_2026-09-18.md) now supplies all five precinct age profiles: median 20-21 in 1910, 21 in 1920, 18-19 in 1930, 18 in 1940, and 25 in 1950. Seven of 1,668 appearances have unread ages; the ranges account for them. At least 90 of 188 residents in 1940 were under 18. These are manuscript research readings, not a constant-boundary series or a count restricted to the built-up settlement.

Source locators, geography notes, household definitions, and the release limit for later individual records are in the [September 18 report](census_deep_dive_2026-09-18.md). Neither modern place file supplies a named Cuervo place count; that does not mean zero residents.

These tables report census county divisions, **not old election precincts**. They do not supply a comparable Cuervo precinct count. Both census volumes are now held; the earlier statement that they were not downloaded is obsolete.

## Limits that must accompany use

1. **Settlement and precinct differ.** Label the series “Cuervo election precinct.” The 1950 district includes rural territory beyond the built-up settlement.
2. **1910–1920 precinct boundaries need reconstruction.** Matching names and numbers do not establish matching territory. The Census Bureau omitted retrospective comparisons after redistricting.
3. **1917 county formation affects the county series.** De Baca County took territory from Guadalupe and other counties. Without territorial population reconstruction, we cannot quantify how much of Guadalupe's reported decline was administrative. The former assertion “mostly administrative” is withdrawn.
4. **The 1950 Guadalupe footnote describes other precinct mergers.** Hailo into precinct 12 and Sombrio into precinct 15 in 1948, and Duoro into precinct 22 in 1945, are not Santa Rosa municipal annexations. No Cuervo/Santa Rosa precinct change is flagged for 1930–50; that does not replace legal boundary history.
5. **Decennial counts cannot identify a paving-year effect.** They do not locate an abrupt 1935/1936 break or measure sales, visitors, employment, or profit. Falling population does not prove no roadside business boom occurred.
6. **Different outcomes do not establish a controlled experiment.** The towns differed in administrative roles, railway functions, business mix, access, and reporting geography. Their populations do not reveal a proven minimum survival threshold.
7. **Growth elsewhere does not rule out harm here.** Santa Rosa's growth cannot establish that I-40 did no damage to Cuervo businesses. Earlier Cuervo decline also does not establish the cause of its later trajectory.
8. **Read the scans.** Several tables have badly misaligned OCR. Source locators and uncertain readings must remain attached to the derived data.
