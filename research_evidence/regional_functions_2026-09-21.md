# Cuervo and Santa Rosa: work, surviving functions, and the population boundary

*Research follow-up, September 21, 2026. A complete first pass through Santa Rosa's 1950 main occupation fields, selected household checks, new roadside and fuel-business evidence, and a reproducible 2010/2020 geographic audit.*

## What this changes

**The evidence increasingly supports studying the loss and relocation of particular town functions while families and the surrounding economy continued.** It does not yet identify how much trade or employment moved from Cuervo to Santa Rosa, or assign a share of the decline to any one transport change.

This pass completed three useful pieces of work:

1. **Santa Rosa's 1950 occupation inventory:** all 87 population sheets in its three municipal enumeration districts have been visually read. The inventory contains **643 main occupation appearances and one additional industry-only entry**. The earlier Cuervo inventory contains 56 main occupation appearances.
2. **Business evidence:** a 1946 guide offers a contemporary comparison of roadside provision; a 2013 visitor reports a working Cuervo gas station; official storage-tank inventories supply specific facility records to pursue. Willa Higgins's shop still lacks operating dates.
3. **Later population geography:** 2010 and 2020 census blocks have been acquired, joined and mapped. The exercise establishes why whole blocks cannot simply be added and labeled the village population. It does not fill the 1960–2000 gap.

The deliverable includes the readings, original source files, source hashes, scripts, figures and explicit unresolved checks. These are research inventories, not official employment statistics.

## 1. A contemporary roadside comparison before the interstate

Jack D. Rittenhouse's *A guide book to highway 66* (1946), printed pages 73–74, describes:

| Place | Provision reported in the guide |
|---|---|
| Cuervo | A few gasoline stations and groceries; approximately a dozen dwellings; “no cafe, garage, or other tourist accommodations.” |
| Newkirk | Four gasoline stations, two lunchrooms and some cabins; DeBaca's Trading Post; no garage. |
| Santa Rosa | Named garages, cafes, hotel and tourist courts, roughly ten additional courts, and a full range of stores and services. |

This establishes an observed service hierarchy by 1946. Newkirk is a useful additional comparison because the same observer reports more roadside facilities there than in Cuervo. The guide's place-population figures have unspecified boundaries and are not added to our census series. Its negative Cuervo observation concerns 1946 and roadside provision; it cannot establish that no Cuervo resident subsequently worked in a cafe.

Source: [Newberry catalog](https://i-share-nby.primo.exlibrisgroup.com/permalink/01CARLI_NBY/i5mcb2/alma996370688805867), call number G89.747. Reading copies: [printed pages 72–73](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/sources/regional_functions_2026-09-21/rittenhouse_1946/b99b026a-2ef9-4995-b8f4-107c50846580/21978e4f91fb7dc4.jpg) and [74–75](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/sources/regional_functions_2026-09-21/rittenhouse_1946/b99b026a-2ef9-4995-b8f4-107c50846580/19e568a5727958a0.jpg). **Interpretation:** substantial differences existed before I-40. This does not establish that the interstate caused no additional harm to Cuervo. A town can enter a transport change with limited services and still lose some of them through that change.

## 2. What residents' recorded work looked like in 1950

|---|---:|---:|
| Agriculture and ranching | 28 | 43 |
| Railroad | 9 | 26 |
| Motor vehicle sales, service and repair | 1 | 63 |
| Restaurants and bars | 3 | 84 |
| Lodging | 0 | 34 |
| Other retail and wholesale | 6 | 92 |
| Construction and building trades | 1 | 66 |
| Government administration and protection | 3 | 45 |
| Education | 3 | 50 |
| Utilities and communications | 0 | 26 |
| Personal, domestic and other services | 1 | 69 |
| Health, finance and professional services | 0 | 18 |
| Other or unresolved industry | 1 | 27 |
| **Main occupation appearances** | **56** | **643** |

Numbers count written main occupations, including some people absent from work or seeking work. They are **not numbers of establishments, unique employed people, or jobs physically located in each place**. The blank-occupation newspaper-office row in Santa Rosa (ED 10-9, sheet 5, line 6) is preserved separately.

### What the comparison supports

- **Cuervo's inventory is heavily concentrated in ranching and rail work:** 37 of 56 entries, or 66.1%. Two of the nine rail entries describe people looking for work. The figure should not be represented as the current rail payroll.
- **Santa Rosa's inventory shows a broader service economy:** motor businesses, food and lodging together account for 181 of 643 entries (28.1%); government and education account for another 95 (14.8%). Neither group is an estimate of revenue from tourists or government spending.
- **Agricultural work and town residence coexist:** 43 agriculture-related entries occur among residents of Santa Rosa's municipal districts. A rural occupation does not require a rural home address. This is especially relevant to our tendency to imagine separate, self-contained economies.
- **Institutional roles deserve individual histories:** the schedules name school employment, postal work, county functions, agricultural administration and other public activities. They give concrete employers and functions to trace, rather than making transportation the explanation for every job.
- **Construction needs caution:** 66 Santa Rosa entries are classified in construction and trades. This snapshot may include temporary projects or a building cycle; it cannot establish a permanent employment base.

### Geography and transcription limits

Cuervo ED 10-6 includes the precinct's ranch hinterland. Santa Rosa's three EDs cover the municipality; the surrounding rural ED 10-11 and Newkirk ED 10-7 are excluded. The difference between an agriculture-heavy rural precinct and a service town is partly built into these geographic choices. The percentages describe these inventories; they do not measure a controlled town-to-town difference.

All pages received a first visual pass, not an independent double transcription. Faint or ambiguous readings remain flagged. Two possible duplicate occupation pairs are retained: Natividad Garcia's dishwasher entries (10-8, sheets 22 and 74) and George M. Clayborn's nightclub-musician entries (10-10, sheet 81). Conflicting ages, marital statuses or dwelling references prevent automatic merging. Removing one from each pair would change the denominator from 643 to 641 and would not erase the broad occupational contrast, but the identities still need resolution.

The ED covers also need population reconciliation: their field totals sum to 2,184 and their Washington totals to 2,205, while the published municipal count is 2,199. We do not divide our occupation inventory by any of these to manufacture an employment rate.

Classification primarily follows the recorded industry. A janitor at a bank is grouped with banking, a school cook with education, and highway construction with construction. Mixed station/court/cafe businesses are flagged; their placement can affect separate sector totals. Some generic family bookkeeping entries use an explicitly flagged inference from the adjacent family business. The Cuervo bookkeeper's unknown industry is left unresolved. Blank fields are not unemployment counts, and the inventory cannot recover all unpaid or unrecorded work.

**What follows:** a fall in one occupation or local service could affect a household that also had another source of work. Conversely, access to employment or schooling elsewhere could make continued residence possible. These are mechanisms to test with dated histories. The schedules alone do not demonstrate commuting destinations, household budgets, or the effect of losing a school, rail job or shop.

The [six Cuervo mixed-job group leads](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/research_evidence/regional_functions_2026-09-21/cuervo_mixed_job_group_leads.json) are a linkage aid. They are not a count of verified independent families with diversified incomes. Santa Rosa's hotel families, lodgers and individual census-report additions require more reconciliation before comparable household percentages would be defensible.

### A reported operating station in 2013

K. Mennem's first-person travel account, published October 1, 2013, describes a working gas station north of I-40 while treating the older settlement to the south as a ghost town. The article says the trip was recent but gives no exact visit date. This is a published visitor observation, not a license inspection, receipt or continuous operating history. ## 6. Why later population requires a boundary before a number

![Extent of blocks intersecting a fixed Cuervo study rectangle](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/research_evidence/regional_functions_2026-09-21/population_boundary_problem.png)

Some blocks that touch the settlement extend many kilometers into the surrounding country. Adding all their residents would count people who may live outside the village. Assigning residents in proportion to land area would assume a distribution we do not observe. A road-corridor block can extend especially far.

I used a fixed reference point at **35.03111° N, 104.40861° W** and explicitly defined four study areas. The rectangle is bounded by longitudes −104.422 to −104.395 and latitudes 35.020 to 35.043, an area of about 6.28 km². It includes the settlement vicinity and some surrounding land; it is not an adopted town boundary. The circular windows test sensitivity to size.

For each year, the lower number sums only blocks entirely inside the window. The upper number also includes the full published population of every block crossing its boundary. Touching at an edge with no positive area is excluded.

| Fixed study area | 2010 allocation bounds | 2020 allocation bounds |
|---|---:|---:|
| Settlement-vicinity rectangle, 6.28 km² | 0–17 | 5–27 |
| Half-mile radius, 2.03 km² | 0–15 | 5–25 |
| Two-mile radius, 32.55 km² | 2–17 | 9–27 |
| Five-mile radius, 203.41 km² | 10–23 | 20–38 |

**These are geographic allocation bounds conditional on published counts. They are not confidence intervals or bounds on the true population.** A zero lower bound means no population was assigned to wholly contained blocks, not that nobody lived in the window. The intervals overlap; even before considering statistical protections, these results cannot establish a 2010–2020 increase. The rectangle permits changes from −12 to +27 under its allocation bounds.

Small 2020 counts also reflect census disclosure-avoidance procedures. The Census Bureau recommends combining small populations/geographies for more reliable analysis. The tables therefore cannot sustain precise claims about a few people arriving, leaving or occupying a particular building. [Census Bureau guidance](https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2024/dec/c2020br-08.html).

### Checks performed and what remains

- The original 2010 statewide TIGER block distribution yields **1,708 Guadalupe County blocks and 4,687 residents**.
- The 2020 geometry contains **902 county blocks**. All were matched by geographic identifier to the separately held DHC geographic file: SUMLEV 100, GEOCOMP 00, GEOVAR 00.
- All 902 blocks' population and housing totals agree between the downloaded TIGER attributes and DHC. County population sums to **4,452** in both. This is a consistency check between releases, not an independent population enumeration.
- The map and allocations use projected coordinates. The script respects the 2010 archive's NAD83 coordinate system and the 2020 query's WGS84 output. It does not split a block's population by acreage.
- The areas represented by individual blocks changed. Identical-looking labels or roughly similar outlines do not make a stable time series automatically.

The [geographic audit](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/research_evidence/regional_functions_2026-09-21/population_geometry_audit.json) contains every included block and housing bound. [Study-window polygons](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/research_evidence/regional_functions_2026-09-21/study_windows.geojson) make the definitions inspectable. [Analysis script](B:/Meridian/Cuervo/research_evidence/regional_functions_2026-09-21/analyze_population_geometry.py).

**The historical population series is still incomplete after 1950.** The 1950 precinct cannot be spliced to one of these modern windows. A useful next reconstruction would delineate the occupied settlement from dated maps/photographs, reconcile census boundaries, and use address or occupancy evidence to distinguish village homes from ranch residences. Postal-area figures must retain their own geography.

## 7. The explanation we can now write—and what would test it

The evidence supports describing Cuervo as part of a regional economy with overlapping residences, occupations and services. Ranching could persist while local retail, education, rail employment and other functions changed or disappeared. Santa Rosa offered a broader mix of work by 1950, and some of its residents worked in agriculture. Particular families combined sectors. Later activity at an interchange could coexist with decay in the older settlement.

**What remains unproved is the transfer:** who bought less in Cuervo, where those purchases went, which workers moved or commuted, and how specific closures changed household choices. The apparent pattern remains compatible with several mechanisms—commercial concentration, changing ranch labor demand, public-service consolidation and different transport effects at different dates.

The most useful next evidence is now more specific:

1. **Date actual businesses.** Use the three NMED facility IDs and parcel matching for fuel sites; dated directories, licenses and advertisements for Willa's shop; merchant records for Bond & Wiest. Distinguish reopening, relocation, succession and final closure.
2. **Observe transactions and employment changes.** Merchant accounts, invoices, payrolls, ranch accounts and freight returns would test where money and work went. Similar-looking population curves cannot do this alone.
3. **Resolve institutional decisions.** School-board and parish records should establish the timing and stated reasons for changes in regular provision. Family attendance elsewhere is not itself a closure decision.
4. **Reconstruct consistent settlement geography.** The modern block audit shows the allocation problem explicitly. It supplies a foundation rather than a falsely precise completed population curve.
5. **Extend household histories.** The new 1950 town inventory provides exact people/employers to pursue. The matching 1940 Santa Rosa work comparison, full person linkage and consistent household denominators remain unfinished.
